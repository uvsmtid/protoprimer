## Operation

Work goes step by step, one suggestion at a time:

*   We never discuss all suggestions together.
*   For each remaining `TODO` suggestion, we review it alone and I (the user) decide to:
    *   approve it - the decision is recorded (`Decision:` line) and the prefix becomes `APPROVED`;
        once the approved change is implemented, the prefix becomes `DONE`;
    *   set it to `IGNORE` - with a short reason recorded in the sub-section;
    *   skip it - it remains `TODO`, and we come back to it later;
    *   `POSTPONE` it - it is understood and wanted, but blocked (e.g. no content exists yet); we return when the blocker is gone.
*   I also make changes manually (outside of this session):
    commits done outside of the session are reviewed (e.g. via `git log`) and the suggestions are updated to match
    (e.g. made `DONE`, adjusted, or dropped when overtaken).
*   New suggestions may appear as a result of that (or of any approved change) - they are appended as new `###` sub-sections.

## Conventions

*   Each suggestion is its own `### <PREFIX>: <title>` sub-section.
    The `<PREFIX>` is its current state (the reason is recorded inside for `IGNORE` and `POSTPONE`):
    *   `TODO` - to be reviewed;
    *   `APPROVED` - decided, but not implemented yet (between `TODO` and `DONE`);
    *   `DONE` - the `APPROVED` change is implemented;
    *   `IGNORE` - will not be done;
    *   `POSTPONE` - wanted, but blocked.
*   `DONE` and `IGNORE` suggestions are removed from this file once nothing is left to do or remember about them (they stay in the git history).
*   Docs are pointed to by several refs (file + heading + quoted phrase), not by line numbers,
    because parts will be moved, split, or renamed.
*   New suggestions may be appended as we progress.
